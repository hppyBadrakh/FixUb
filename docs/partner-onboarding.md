# Working with FixUB: for partner organizations

For the manager of an organization that will receive reports through FixUB. It says what
FixUB does, what it asks of you, and how to start.

## What FixUB does

Residents photograph a problem in a public place (a pothole, a broken street light), mark it on
the map and describe it. The FixUB team checks each report, sets its priority and assigns it
to the organization responsible. Your staff see it in their queue, update its status as they
work, and mark it resolved. The resident, and anyone else, can follow that on a public page.

FixUB does not repair anything and does not decide your priorities. It makes sure a report
reaches the right people and that what happens next is visible.

## What we ask of you

1. **A manager** who is responsible for your queue and can close or reopen reports.
2. **Staff accounts.** Each person signs in once with a Google account; we then give them
   access to your organization (and to a repair unit, if you use units).
3. **Statuses kept up to date.** When work starts, when it is done. A short public note helps
   residents more than anything else.
4. **Agreed response times** by priority. Our starting proposal, counted from the moment the
   priority is set: critical 2 days, high 7, medium 14, low 30. Reports past the agreed time
   are shown as overdue to you and to us (not to the public).
5. **A second look when residents disagree.** After "resolved", the person who reported and
   those who said "me too" can answer whether it is actually fixed. If more say "still broken"
   than "fixed", your manager is notified. It does not change the status: you decide.

## What you get

- Reports with a photo, an exact location and a category, already checked and prioritized.
- Duplicates caught before they reach you where possible, and merged when they do.
- One queue for your organization, sorted by urgency, usable on a phone.
- Numbers you can show: how many reports, how fast the first response, how many resolved.
- Proof of work: a completion photo on the public page when a repair is done.

## What your staff see and don't see

- They see the reports assigned to your organization, and the public map like everyone else.
- They do **not** see who filed a report. Nobody outside the FixUB team's database
  administrators can, and they only look when abuse has to be investigated.
- They see the priority and an internal note from the FixUB team. The note is never public.

## How to start

1. Tell us the organization's name (Mongolian and English), its repair units if any, and which
   categories of problem you handle.
2. Name the manager and the staff. Each signs in at the site with Google once.
3. We set up the organization and give everyone their access. The manager checks the queue
   opens on their phone.
4. We agree the response times and a weekly time to look at overdue and disputed reports together.
5. First reports are assigned.

## Points to settle in writing

Not a legal text: a list of what a memorandum between us should cover. Have it drafted or
checked by a lawyer.

- Which area and which categories of problem you take on.
- The response times, and what "responded" means (a status change with a note).
- That statuses and public notes are published, with the organization's name, not a person's.
- Who the contact people are on both sides, and how fast each answers the other.
- What happens to reports outside your responsibility (we close them as "out of scope" or assign elsewhere).
- How long the arrangement runs and how either side ends it.
- Personal data: you receive none about the people who report; the photos and locations are
  public information once published.
